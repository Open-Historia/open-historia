# Puppets are their own ledger, and their secrecy is per-row

A Puppet is a polity whose will is directed by an Overlord while it remains a separate country, holding its own territory and its own sovereignty. That fact lives in a new engine-owned array, `world.puppets`, alongside `wars`, `relations` and `agreements`, and each row carries its own `secrecy` (`open` or `covert`) plus a `knownTo[]` list of who has learned a covert one. `loyalty` is on the row too, and is hidden from everyone but the Overlord in every case, including an openly announced protectorate.

Both halves of this follow from one reading of the domain: a subordination is a **directional, partly secret fact about a pair of polities**. Direction rules out the symmetric containers; partial secrecy rules out treating every Puppet the same way.

Neither half is cheap to undo. The ledger becomes save data the moment a game writes a row, and `secrecy` decides what the panel, the map overlay, the advisor prompt and the chat task are each allowed to say — moving it later means touching all four and re-deciding what existing saves meant.

## Considered Options

- **A new `type` in the existing `agreements` ledger.** Rejected. `agreements.parties[]` is symmetric with nowhere to record which party directs the other, its `status` vocabulary (`active`/`suspended`/`ended`/`expired`) has no room for Loyalty, and every instrument in that list is a public one — a covert client has no home there. The directional `guarantor`/`beneficiary` fields show the shape can be bent, but bending it here would mean a second, contradictory reading of what an agreement is.
- **An `overlord` pointer on the polity in `polityOverrides`.** Rejected. `polityOverrides` is identity — name, colour, aliases — and is re-keyed wholesale on rename by `renamePolityInWorld`. A pointer stored there would need its own rename migration, which matters precisely because renaming your Puppet is one of the powers this feature grants. A ledger row resolves its names through `polityIdentity.js`, exactly as `wars` and `relations` already do, and folds onto one identity when a polity is renamed.
- **Every Puppet is covert.** Rejected. A protectorate is a signed, published treaty and a Warsaw Pact satellite was known to every government on earth; under a blanket rule the model would hold a subordination secret while narrating the treaty that created it. What is actually secret is the tightness of the leash and the resentment underneath it — which is why Loyalty is hidden unconditionally and the arrangement is not.
- **Every Puppet is public.** Rejected. It removes the covert client entirely, and with it the reason for espionage to care.
- **Mirror the projects board's `verification` machinery so a turned agent can fabricate a Puppet.** Rejected. It would mean false rows in a ledger the simulator reads as truth, and doubles a state machine for one trick. Staleness buys most of the same drama for nothing: `knownTo[]` is never pruned when a relationship ends, so a polity that learned something in 1948 goes on believing it after it lapsed, and nothing corrects them until fresh reporting does.
- **An `impacts.puppetOps` array the model fills directly.** Rejected, in line with the existing ledgers. The directors exist so a model cannot assert a diplomatic fact the world does not support; a free-form array would let one invent a subordination between two polities that have never met.

## Consequences

- The advisor's context gains its first field filtered **by what the player has learned**. Two weaker forms of this already exist and should be read first: field-level omission (the advisor is simply never handed storylines, spies, intercepts or projects) and row-level filtering by *participation* (`chatVisibility.js`, which gives each leader only the chats that polity was in). `knownTo[]` is the step past both — participation is answerable from data already present, whereas acquired knowledge is state the engine accumulates over time and which can be stale. Note also that `chatVisibility.js` makes the advisor's filter a deliberate no-op, since the player is in every chat: nothing has ever actually been withheld from the advisor at row level, and this would be the first. The payoff is that the advisor keeps its contract — it knows everything the player knows and nothing more — and will confidently repeat stale beliefs.
- One shared `visiblePuppetsFor(world, viewer)` backs the Country panel, the map overlay, the advisor prompt and the chat task. Four callers computing the same visibility rule separately would drift, which is the failure `countryTags.js` was extracted to prevent.
- Loyalty is AI-owned like `relations.score`, with one deterministic engine rule layered on: a refused demand costs Loyalty. Without it, the single interaction the player directly drives would move the score only when the model remembered to emit a line.
- Chains are banned rather than modelled. Installing a Puppet that already holds Puppets **reparents** them to the new Overlord in the same event, permanently — releasing the middle party later does not give them back.
- `client-state` and `puppet-state` leave `TAG_SUGGESTIONS`. The vocabulary is open, so a model can still write either as free text, but nothing steers it there and the ledger is the only place the question is answered.
- Existing saves get an empty array, with no migration. `migrateLegacyDiplomaticState` could seed relations from treaty events because a treaty says plainly that two countries signed something; "A secretly directs B" is an inference from narrative, and guessing it wrong would write a false subordination into a long campaign with no way for the player to know it was invented.
