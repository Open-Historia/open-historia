Get Started with Open Historia

Free and open source. The game runs on your own machine, with an AI provider you choose — here is how to get it running and play your first game.

Three Ways to Play

Desktop app — recommended

The desktop app is how the game is meant to be played: the world map is served from your own machine, so it is fast and works offline. Get it from the desktop release:

  • Windows 10 & 11: run Open-Historia-Setup.exe. If SmartScreen warns, choose More info → Run anyway — the installer is not code-signed yet.
  • macOS: download the Apple Silicon or Intel zip, unzip, and drag Open Historia to Applications. The first time, right-click it and choose Open.
  • Linux: install the .deb on Debian, Ubuntu and derivatives, or make the AppImage executable and run it.

Nothing else needs installing. On first launch the app downloads the world map data once (about 275 MB, with a progress bar). Your games and scenarios are saved on your computer.

In your browser — a demo

Press Play on openhistoria.com to try the game without installing anything. The browser version streams every map tile from the community content-node network, so expect noticeable lag when panning and zooming — it is there to try the game, not the best way to play it.

Your games are saved in that browser, on that device. Signing in with Google is optional; it syncs your games and scenarios between devices, encrypted before they leave your browser. Your AI key goes straight from your browser to your provider.

Android

Download open-historia.apk from the Android release and open it to install (allow installs from your browser when Android asks). It is the browser version packaged as an app: it connects to a content node on its own and streams the world map from there, and your games are saved on the phone. There is no server to run and nothing to point it at.

Prefer to build from source, or run the server on another machine? See the self-hosting guide.

You Need an AI Provider

Open Historia has no scripted simulation: every time skip, every event, every border change and every leader's reply is generated by a language model. Without a connected AI provider the world cannot advance — you can look at the map and use the map editor, but not play.

Open a game, open Settings, and pick a provider under AI. Gemini is the default; a free key from Google AI Studio is enough to start. OpenAI, Anthropic, and anything that speaks the OpenAI or Anthropic APIs — including local models through Ollama or LM Studio — work too. The key is stored in the app and sent only to the provider you chose. See the AI setup guide for every option.

Your First Game

Step 1 — The main menu

The menu has three tabs: Games (your saves), Scenarios (the worlds you can start from, with their editor), and Community (the Scenario Hub).

Step 2 — New Game

Click New Game. Modern Day (starting 1 January 2016) is the built-in scenario; others can be imported from the Community tab first. Click the country you want to lead on the picker map, then choose a difficulty from Very Easy to Impossible.

Step 3 — Look around

Click any country on the world map. Its panel shows what the map-maker wrote about it, an AI intelligence briefing on request, and an Open Diplomacy button that starts a chat with its leader.

Step 4 — Act

Type what your nation does in the Actions panel in plain language — mobilise, sign a treaty, fund a programme, move troops. Actions queue up and are resolved at the next time skip. Negotiate with other leaders in chat, ask the Advisor for a briefing or a plan, and deploy spies from the chat panel.

Step 5 — Skip time

Click the date at the top of the screen and choose how far to jump — from 6 hours to 1 year. The AI resolves your queued actions, plays every other nation, writes the events of that period and moves borders and fronts. Read the events on the timeline, then act again.

Good to Know

  • Single-player: you lead one nation and the AI plays the rest. There is no multiplayer lobby. On a self-hosted server other devices on your network can open the same saves — see the self-hosting guide.
  • Saves: the game saves itself as you play — on your computer in the desktop app, on the phone in the Android app, and in the browser in the demo.
  • Scenario Hub: browse and import community scenarios from the Community tab, and publish your own.
  • Map editor: every scenario opens in a full vector map editor — redraw borders, paint owners, place cities, then Apply & Play.
