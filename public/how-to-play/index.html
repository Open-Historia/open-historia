How to Play Open Historia

From your first look at the interactive world map to negotiating with AI leaders, giving orders in plain language, skipping time, building custom worlds in the map editor, and sharing them with the community.

Overview: What Is Open Historia?

Open Historia is a free, open-source grand strategy game where you lead one nation on an interactive world map. You give orders in plain language, negotiate with AI-run nations through chat, deploy troops and spies, and then skip time — hours, days, months or a year — while an AI simulates how the rest of the world responds: events, wars, treaties, and borders that actually move.

It is built as a community alternative to Pax Historia, with a full vector map editor, a Scenario Hub for sharing custom worlds, a desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux, an Android app, and a browser demo.

The AI Runs the World

There is no scripted simulation underneath. Every time skip is written by a language model from the current state of the world: what happened, who gained or lost territory, how other nations reacted. You bring the model — Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic, or a local one — and the game sends it your key directly. Without an AI provider connected the world cannot advance, so set one up first: see the AI setup guide.

Core Features & Mechanics

Interactive World Map

The map is your primary interface. Pan, zoom, and click any region, city or unit to inspect it. Click a nation to open its panel: the description its map-maker wrote, an AI intelligence briefing on request, and an Open Diplomacy button. Watch territory, borders, and nations shift as history unfolds after every time skip.

AI Diplomacy

Open a chat with any nation's leader and talk to them — literally. Propose alliances, demand territory, threaten, broker peace, or just probe their intentions; the leader answers in character, shaped by your relationship, relative strength, and the state of the world. What is agreed in chat feeds into the next time skip.

Before engaging, ask for an AI intelligence briefing on any nation from its panel to understand its strength, economy, allies, and posture.

Actions in Plain Language

The Actions panel is where you govern. Type what your nation does — "mobilise the reserves along the northern border", "offer Kenya a trade agreement", "fund a nuclear programme" — and it joins the queue. Queued actions are resolved at the next time skip, when the AI works out what they achieve and how everyone else responds.

AI-Generated Events

Each time skip produces dynamic events shaped by your decisions and the evolving world: a booming economy attracts investment, an aggressive neighbour provokes a border crisis, a neglected province rebels. Events land on the timeline with dates and consequences; you respond to them with actions, diplomacy, and troops.

AI Advisor

Stuck on strategy? Consult your AI advisor for situation summaries, economic analysis, and war planning. The advisor reads the current game state and offers concrete recommendations — which nation to court, where to position troops, how to steady a crumbling economy.

Spies

From the chat panel you can deploy spies to other nations. Agents in place intercept what their host is saying to other leaders and report back; foreign agents caught in your own country can be expelled or turned into double agents. Espionage carries risk — an exposed operation sours relations.

Troops & Combat

You deploy, move, and position armies on the map. Deployments stay pending until the AI resolves them at the next time skip — you set your orders, and the AI decides the outcome from the strength of the forces involved, your other actions, and the wider situation, moving fronts and borders accordingly. Scenarios control which troop types exist in their era (infantry, cavalry, tanks, aircraft, and so on).

Time Skips

Click the date at the top of the screen and choose how far to jump: 6 hours, 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, or 1 year. Short skips give fine control during a crisis; long ones let whole eras play out. The AI then resolves your queued actions, plays every other nation, and writes the period's events onto the timeline.

Using the Map Editor

Open Historia includes a full vector map editor built into the scenario editor. You can:

  • Draw regions: Create new territories with freehand drawing tools
  • Split and merge borders: Redraw the political map however you want
  • Paint owners: Assign regions to nations, with their colours and flags
  • Place cities: Search and import from a database of around 70,000 world places, or place them by hand
  • Credit your work: Name and author your map before you share it
  • Apply & Play: Hit one button to switch from editing to playing on your new map

Open the editor from any scenario in the library by clicking Open Map Editor, or visit /?editor=1 for the standalone editor.

Scenarios & The Scenario Hub

Modern Day, starting on 1 January 2016, is the only built-in scenario. Everything else lives on the Scenario Hub, reached from the in-game Community tab. The official presets there include:

  • World War II — 1939 — global war from the German invasion of Poland
  • Medieval — 1200 AD — feudal Europe, crusades, and medieval empires
  • Rome — 117 AD — the Roman Empire at its greatest extent
  • Mongol World — 1300 AD — the Mongol Empire and its neighbours
  • New World — 1650 — colonial powers and global trade
  • Bronze Age — 1200 BC — the Late Bronze Age on the eve of the Collapse

Alongside them are community scenarios such as The Great War — 1911, The Cold War — 1946, Fault Lines — 2014, and the Second Punic War — 218 BC. Browse them sorted by imports, likes, or date, import any with one click, and publish your own from the same tab. Each scenario brings its own map, starting conditions, troop types, and historical context.

Gameplay Loop

A typical round in Open Historia looks like this:

  1. Survey: Read the latest events on the timeline and check your nation's situation — economy, military, relations.
  2. Consult the Advisor: Ask for a briefing or a plan based on the current situation.
  3. Diplomacy: Chat with the leaders that matter — negotiate, threaten, ally, or gather intelligence.
  4. Act: Queue your nation's actions in plain language and position your troops.
  5. Skip time: Choose how far to jump. The AI resolves your actions, plays the other nations, and writes the events of that period.
  6. Repeat: React to what happened and plan the next move.

Tips for New Players

  • Start with Modern Day: The built-in scenario is the most polished and the easiest to learn on.
  • Use a capable model: The quality of events, diplomacy, and advice is the quality of the model you connect. Small local models struggle with the large answer a time skip needs.
  • Skip short at first: A few days at a time shows you how the world responds to each action before you commit to a year.
  • Use the AI advisor often: It reads the whole game state and is the fastest way to understand your position.
  • Diplomacy is powerful: A well-timed alliance can be worth more than an expensive war.
  • Read the briefings: Before engaging any nation, get an intelligence briefing to understand its strength.
  • Try the map editor: Even if you never publish, editing a map teaches you how territory and borders work.
