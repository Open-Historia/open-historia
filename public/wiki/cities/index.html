    Cities and Structures

    Cities on the map and the bases, ports and landmarks that get built during a campaign.

    On this page

    Two kinds of thing are drawn on top of the political map: cities, which come with the world, and structures, which get built during a campaign.

    Cities

    Cities come from the scenario. Modern Day uses a stock set of roughly seventy thousand; a custom scenario can ship its own, or import from the same database.

    They appear from about zoom 3.4 and get denser as you zoom in. Click one for its name, population, whether it is a capital, and its tier.

    Cities change

    The world can rename a city and change its population, and it does both for real reasons:

    Renaming happens when a city is renamed after a leader or an ideology, when a capital is re-designated, or when a conqueror gives a captured city their own name. It works on any city on the map, including ones that shipped with the world rather than being authored by a scenario.

    Population moves when something plausibly moves it — a siege, a famine, an epidemic, a bombing or an evacuation shrinking a city; an industrial boom, a resettlement programme or a refugee influx growing one. The world always writes the new total, not the change.

    That means a long campaign leaves a visible demographic trace on the map, which is one of the quieter pleasures of playing one.

    Structures

    Structures are markers placed during play: military bases, ports, embassies, airfields, research facilities, landmarks — the kind is free-form, so the world names whatever it needs.

    Each has a name, a kind, an owner, a position, and optionally a note and a founding date.

    Structures never move borders

    This is the rule worth knowing. A structure's owner is whoever runs the facility, not who owns the ground it stands on.

    An American airbase in Germany does not make that region American. An embassy is not a piece of the sending country. Building a port in an allied country annexes nothing.

    If you want the ground, that is a territorial change, and it has to happen as one.

    Building and losing them

    Structures appear when an event founds or constructs one, and disappear when one is destroyed. Building something with the same name as an existing structure replaces it rather than stacking a second one on the same spot.

    You get structures by ordering them — "begin construction of a naval base at Gdynia" — and by the world building its own. Watching foreign bases appear near your borders is a genuinely useful early-warning system.

    Placing them yourself

    The cheats panel has map-feature tools: edit an existing city or structure, add a new one, or clear out ones that have accumulated and stopped mattering.

    Adding your first custom city to a scenario flips it into custom-cities mode, after which the scenario carries its own city list rather than the stock one.

    For authoring a whole world's cities, use the map editor, which can import from the full city database or place them by hand.
