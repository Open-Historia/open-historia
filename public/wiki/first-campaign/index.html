    Your First Campaign

    A guided first hour — from the main menu to your first war, told step by step.

    On this page

    This walks through the first hour of a campaign as Poland in the Modern Day scenario, on Medium. Poland is a good first country: it has real neighbours with real opinions about it, it is not so powerful that nothing threatens it, and not so weak that everything does.

    Follow along with your own country if you prefer — the shape is the same.

    Before you start

    Make sure you have connected an AI provider. Without one, the world will not respond and this walkthrough will not work.

    1. Start the game

    From the main menu, Scenarios → Modern Day → New Game.

    The country picker opens with a map and a searchable list. Find Poland and select it. Leave the Faction tab alone for now — that is for inventing a country that does not exist.

    Pick ⚖️ Medium. Start.

    The map loads centred on your country, with your flag in the corner and the date pill top right.

    2. Find out where you stand

    Open the advisor — the 🧭 button on the right edge. Ask it:

    What are the three biggest risks to Poland right now?

    It reads the actual world state and answers about it, sometimes with a chart. This is the fastest way to understand a position you have just inherited.

    Now open the Stats tab in the same drawer. You get your economy, your stability, your strategic indices and your intelligence rating. Note the intelligence number — it decides how well your spies work and how well you can read other people's, and 40 is ordinary.

    Finally, click Germany on the map. Its country panel opens: flag, tags, recent events, and a button for an intelligence briefing. Read it. Do the same for Russia and Ukraine.

    3. Talk to someone

    Click Germany, then open a chat.

    Say something with an actual ask in it:

    Chancellor, Poland wants firmer guarantees on the eastern flank. What would Berlin want in return for backing a permanent NATO presence on our territory?

    You will get a reply in the German leader's voice, shaped by the current state of the world and by how Germany sees you. Keep going for a few messages. Notice that they remember what you said earlier in the thread.

    Diplomacy here is not a menu of treaty buttons. If you want an alliance, you have to talk someone into one.

    4. Write your orders

    Open the actions panel (✦ in the bottom toolbar) and queue two or three things. For a first turn as Poland, something like:

    • Open formal negotiations with Germany on a permanent allied presence in eastern Poland.
    • Begin a two-year programme to expand domestic ammunition production.
    • Quietly increase intelligence collection on Russian force movements near Kaliningrad.

    Three orders is plenty. Each one should be a thing a government could actually decide to do this month.

    If you are not sure how to phrase one, type a rough version and press the ✦ sparkle to have it rewritten into a proper order. There is also a suggestions button that proposes orders based on your situation.

    5. Skip a month

    Open the time panel (» on the date pill) and choose 1 month.

    This takes a little while — the model is simulating the world. You can cancel if it hangs.

    6. Read what happened

    Events reveal one at a time. Step through them with Next event. Watch the map: borders, units and markers change as each event lands, and the camera moves to whatever the event is about.

    Look for:

    • Whether your orders were picked up — an event should reference the negotiation you opened.
    • What other countries did on their own. They are not waiting for you.
    • Anything that has clearly started rather than finished. A crisis that opens this turn is the thing to pay attention to next turn.

    7. Do it again, but respond

    Now the campaign is actually running. Second turn:

    • Go back to the advisor and ask what changed.
    • Reply to any leader who wrote to you — countries will open chats with you unprompted.
    • Write orders that respond to the events rather than repeating your last three.

    Take another month. Then another. Somewhere in the first handful of turns something will go wrong in a way you did not plan for, and that is when the game starts being interesting.

    Common first-hour mistakes

    Skipping a whole year on turn one. You will get a compressed summary of twelve months and lose all the texture. Start with months.

    Writing one vague order. "Improve the economy" produces a vague event. Name the policy.

    Ignoring the chats. Countries write to you. If you never answer, you are playing a much smaller game than the one on offer.

    Never using Undo. A turn that goes absurdly is a turn worth rewinding. It is in the time panel and it is there to be used.

    Trying to control your army directly. On the stable build, hands-on troop deployment lives in the cheats panel — the intended way to move forces is to order it and let the world carry it out. See military and combat.
