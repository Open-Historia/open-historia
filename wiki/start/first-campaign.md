This walks through the first hour of a campaign as **Poland in the Modern Day scenario**, on
Medium. Poland is a good first country: it has real neighbours with real opinions about it, it is
not so powerful that nothing threatens it, and not so weak that everything does.

Follow along with your own country if you prefer — the shape is the same.

## Before you start

Make sure you have [connected an AI provider](/wiki/ai-setup/). Without one, the world will not
respond and this walkthrough will not work.

## 1. Start the game

From the main menu, **Scenarios → Modern Day → New Game**.

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta the menu may be behind a vault door.</b> The first time the game
starts after an update, a steel door stands before the main menu: click it and it opens.
Settings has a switch to show it every time.</p>

The country picker opens with a map and a searchable list. Find **Poland** and select it. Leave
**Create a faction** alone for now — that is for inventing a country that does not exist.

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta the picker has two tabs.</b> <b>Pick a country</b> is the map and
the list, each country with its colour on the map and its flag where one is known; <b>Play as a
group</b> is for leading a group instead — a cartel, a militia, a movement — one of the
scenario's or one you make up, and inventing a faction of your own is there too. Stay on Pick a
country.</p>

Pick **⚖️ Medium**, and the game starts.

The map loads centred on your country, with your flag in the corner and the date pill top right.
If you have not set up a model yet, the **Set up your AI provider** prompt appears now — paste
your key there and press **Save and play**.

## 2. Find out where you stand

**Open the advisor** — the button in the bottom-right corner. Ask it:

> What are the three biggest risks to Poland right now?

It reads the actual world state and answers about it, sometimes with a chart. This is the
fastest way to understand a position you have just inherited.

Now **open the Stats tab** in the same drawer. You get your relations and agreements, your
economy, your stability, your strategic indices and your intelligence rating. Note the
intelligence number — it decides how well your spies work and how well you can read other
people's, and 40 is ordinary.

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta the statistics have a panel of their own.</b> The advisor's
drawer is the advisor alone: your flag in the corner opens your country panel, with the same
statistics and a <b>Ranking</b> tab that sets the countries side by side.</p>

Finally, **click Germany on the map**. A card opens naming the country and the region you
clicked; press **ⓘ** for Germany's panel — flag, details, related events, and an **Advisor
Report** on request. Read it. Do the same for Russia and Ukraine.

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta the world posts.</b> The third button of the toolbar opens
<b>Rostra</b>, a social network where leaders, generals, journalists and celebrities post as time
passes, and where you can post as your country's leader. It costs no AI request.</p>

## 3. Talk to someone

Click **Germany** on the map, then **💬** on its card to open a chat.

Say something with an actual ask in it:

> Chancellor, Poland wants firmer guarantees on the eastern flank. What would Berlin want in
> return for backing a permanent NATO presence on our territory?

You will get a reply in the German leader's voice, shaped by the current state of the world and
by how Germany sees you. Keep going for a few messages. Notice that they remember what you said
earlier in the thread.

Diplomacy here is not a menu of treaty buttons. If you want an alliance, you have to talk
someone into one.

## 4. Write your orders

Open the **actions** panel (✦ in the bottom toolbar). First, set a **standing goal** at the top
— the direction behind your orders, such as *"Keep Poland out of any war while tying Germany and
the United States to our eastern flank."* Your advisor and the simulation steer by it; no other
country ever sees it.

Then queue two or three orders. For a first turn as Poland, something like:

- *Open formal negotiations with Germany on a permanent allied presence in eastern Poland.*
- *Begin a two-year programme to expand domestic ammunition production.*
- *Quietly increase intelligence collection on Russian force movements near Kaliningrad.*

Three orders is plenty. Each one should be a thing a government could actually decide to do this
month.

If you are not sure how to phrase one, type a rough version and press the **✦ sparkle** in the
input (**Improve action text**) to have it rewritten into a proper order. **AI Suggestions**
proposes orders based on your situation, and **Brainstorm** opens the advisor to plan a set of
orders with you.

## 5. Skip a month

Open the time panel (**»** on the date pill) and choose **1 month**.

This takes a little while — the model is simulating the world, and the time panel says what it
is doing. You can cancel if it hangs. Under the skip buttons, the panel also counts the AI
requests you have used today; a skip uses one.

## 6. Read what happened

The Events panel opens and fills as the model writes. Step through the events with **Next
event**. Watch the map: borders, units and markers change as each event lands, and the camera
moves to whatever the event is about. If an event is something you would have reacted to,
**✋ Intervene here** stops the round there, and the rest never happens.

Look for:

- Whether your orders were **picked up** — an event should reference the negotiation you opened.
- What **other countries did** on their own. They are not waiting for you.
- Anything that has clearly **started rather than finished**. A crisis that opens this turn is
  the thing to pay attention to next turn.

## 7. Do it again, but respond

Now the campaign is actually running. Second turn:

- Go back to the advisor and ask what changed.
- Reply to any leader who wrote to you — countries will open chats with you unprompted.
- Write orders that *respond* to the events rather than repeating your last three.

Take another month. Then another. Somewhere in the first handful of turns something will go
wrong in a way you did not plan for, and that is when the game starts being interesting.

## Common first-hour mistakes

**Skipping a whole year on turn one.** You will get a compressed summary of twelve months and
lose all the texture. Start with months.

**Writing one vague order.** *"Improve the economy"* produces a vague event. Name the policy.

**Ignoring the chats.** Countries write to you. If you never answer, you are playing a much
smaller game than the one on offer.

**Never using Undo.** A turn that goes absurdly is a turn worth rewinding. It is in the time
panel and it is there to be used.

**Trying to control your army directly.** Forces move because you *order* it and the world
carries it out; the engine keeps them moving turn after turn. Hands-on deployment exists, but it
is the **Force Manager** in the cheats panel. See [military and combat](/wiki/military/).

<p class="beta-note"><b>On beta a war on your border is yours to command.</b> When you are at war
with a neighbour, a red <b>War Room</b> button appears: speak to your generals, or pick troops and
draw the route they take. Your typed orders still reach them in the same skip.</p>
